Species interaction, types - extinct endangered, endemic, and 
rare species 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Introduction 
 Species form the building blocks of every ecosystem.  
 Each species plays a specific role —whether as a producer, consumer, decomposer, or 
predator. 
 Their interactions help maintain the balance of natural processes like nutrient cycling, 
pollination, and food webs. 
 Loss of even a single species can disrupt ecosystem stability. 
 Biodiverse ecosystems are more resilient to environmental changes and stresses. 
 Understanding species and their roles is crucial for promoting sustainability and ecological 
health. 
What is Species Interaction? 
 Species interaction refers to the relationships and effects organisms have on one another 
within an ecosystem. 
 These interactions can be direct or indirect, short-term or long-term. 
 They include cooperation, competition, feeding, and dependency among different 
species. 
 Plants rely on pollinators and dispersers to reproduce and spread.  


=== page 2 ===
 Herbivores feed on plants, while predators hunt herbivores or other prey. 
 Decomposers like fungi and bacteria break down dead matter and recycle nutrients back 
into the ecosystem. 
 
Why Study Species Interaction? 
 Understanding species interaction helps us assess the health and function of ecosystems.  
 It is crucial for designing sustainable and eco -friendly engineering projects.  
 Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs) depend on knowledge of local species and their 
relationships. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Engineers can minimize harm to biodiversity by identifying key specie s and interaction 
networks. 
 Conservation engineering uses this knowledge to restore and maintain habitats.  
 Species interaction knowledge directly supports long -term sustainable development 
goals. 
Types of Species Interactions 
 Species interact in various ways that affect survival, reproduction, and resource use.  
 The five major types of interactions are mutualism, commensalism, parasitism, predation, 
and competition. 
 Mutualism benefits both species involved. 
 Commensalism benefits one species without affecting  the other. 


=== page 3 ===
 Parasitism benefits one species at the expense of another. 
 Predation and competition  involve one species harming or outcompeting another for 
resources or survival. 
Mutualism 
 Mutualism is a type of species interaction where both organisms benefi t. 
 It is a cooperative relationship that enhances survival, growth, or reproduction.  
 In this interaction, neither species is harmed.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 A common example is bees and flowering plants: bees get nectar for food, and plants 
receive pollination services. 
 Another example is mycorrhizal fungi, which help plant roots absorb nutrients while 
receiving carbohydrates in return. 
 Mutualism is essential for nutrient cycling, pollination, and ecosystem productivity.  
Commensalism 
 Commensalism is a type of interaction where one species benefits while the other is 
neither helped nor harmed. 
 It is a neutral relationship for one species and a positive one for the other.  
 The unaffected species remains unchanged in its survival or behavior. 
 A well-known example is barnacles attaching to whales; the barnacles gain mobility and 
food particles, while whales remain unaffected.  
 Another example is cattle egrets feeding on insects stirred up by grazing cattle.  


=== page 4 ===
 Commensalism often involves shelter, transportation, or feeding advantages. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Parasitism 
 Parasitism is an interaction where one species benefits at the expense of another.  
 The organism that benefits is called the parasite, and the one that is harmed is the host.  
 Parasites rely on their host for food, shelter, or reproduction, often weakening the host 
over time. 
 An example is ticks feeding on the blood of mammals, which can cause irritation and 
transmit diseases. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Another example is parasitic wasps that lay eggs inside other insects, eventually killing the 
host. 


=== page 5 ===
 Parasitism can be external (ectoparasites) or internal (endoparasites), depending on 
where the parasite lives. 
Predation 
 Predation is an interaction where one organism (the predator) kills and eats another (the 
prey). 
 It is a natural mechanism that helps regulate population sizes in ecosystems. 
 Predators are usually well-adapted for hunting, with traits like sharp teeth, claws, or keen 
senses. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 An example is a tiger hunting and feeding on a deer in the forest.  
 Another example is a hawk capturing and eating a mouse in a grassland.  
 Predation plays a key role in maintaining ecological balance and species diversity. 
Slide 10: Competition 
 Competition is an interaction where two or more species compete for the same limited 
resources. 
 These resources can include food, water, space, light, or nutrients. 
 Competition can occur between individuals of the same species (intraspecific) or different 
species (interspecific). 
 It often leads to the dominance of one species and the decline or displacement of the 
other. 
 


=== page 6 ===
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 A common example is plants competing for sunlight in dense forests.  
 Competition influences species distribution, behavior, and evolution over time.  
Types of Species Based on Conservation Status  
 In addition to how species interact, it's import ant to understand their risk of survival. 
 Conservation status indicates how likely a species is to survive in the wild.  
 Species are classified based on population size, habitat condition, and extinction risk.  
 The four key categories include Extinct, Endangered, Endemic, and Rare species. 
 These categories help prioritize conservation efforts and allocate resources effectively. 
 Understanding these types is essential for protecting biodiversity and maintaining 
ecosystem stability. 
Extinct Species – Definition 
 An extinct species is one that no longer exists anywhere on Earth.  
 This means there are no living individuals of that species left, either in the wild or in 
captivity. 
 Extinction is a natural process but has been greatly accelerated by human activities.  
 Once a species goes extinct, its unique role in the ecosystem is lost forever. 
 Extinction can lead to imbalances in food chains and ecosystem functions.  
 Preventing extinction is a major goal of global conservation efforts.  
Causes of Extinction 


=== page 7 ===
 Species extinction is often driven by human-induced environmental changes. 
 Habitat loss due to deforestation, urbanization, and agriculture is a major cause.  
 Climate change alters temperature and weather patterns, making habitats unlivable for 
some species. 
 Hunting and poaching have led to the extinction of several species for meat, fur, or trade. 
 Invasive species outcompete native species for food and space, sometimes wiping them 
out. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 These threats act together, accelerating the extinction rate beyond natural levels. 
Examples of Extinct Species 
 Several species have gone extinct due to human activity and ecological imbalance.  
 The Dodo, native to Mauritius, became extinct in the 1600s due to hunting and invasive 
animals. 
 The Passenger pigeon, once abundant in North America, went extinct by the early 1900s 
due to mass hunting and habitat destruction.  
 The Indian cheetah , once found in parts of India, became extinct locally due to 
overhunting and habitat loss. 
 These extinctions show how human influence can rapidly w ipe out even widespread 
species. 


=== page 8 ===
 Learning from these losses is vital for protecting today ’s threatened species. 
Impact of Species Extinction  
 The extinction of a species causes ripple effects throughout the ecosystem.  
 One major consequence is the disruption of food chains , affecting predators, prey, and 
decomposers. 
 If a key species disappears, other dependent species may also decline or collapse.  
 Extinction also leads to the loss of ecological services like pollination, seed dispersal, and 
nutrient cycling. 
 These services are essential for agriculture, clean water, and climate regulation.  
 Ultimately, species extinction weakens ecosystem stability and reduces its ability to 
support life. 
 Endangered Species – Definition 
 Endangered species are organisms that f ace a very high risk of extinction in the near 
future. 
 Their population sizes are critically low or rapidly declining.  
 These species are often on the brink due to habitat destruction, pollution, or human 
exploitation. 
 The loss of endangered species can lead to severe ecological consequences.  
 Many of them play key roles in maintaining food chains and ecosystem balance.  
 Timely conservation action is essential to prevent their extinction.  
Causes for Endangered Status 
 Many species become endangered due to increasing human pressures on natural 
environments. 
 Human encroachment  involves converting forests, wetlands, and grasslands into cities, 
farms, and roads. 
 Pollution introduces harmful substances into ecosystems, affecting species ’ health and 
reproduction. 
 Habitat fragmentation  splits ecosystems into smaller, disconnected areas, isolating 
species populations. 

=== page 9 ===
 These factors reduce food availability, breeding grounds, and shelter. 
 Long-term exposure to these threats makes species more likely to decline toward  
extinction. 
Examples of Endangered Species in India  
 India is home to several endangered species due to rapid environmental and human 
pressures. 
 The Bengal tiger, India's national animal, faces threats from habitat loss and poaching.  
 The Indian vulture  population has declined drastically due to diclofenac poisoning from 
livestock carcasses. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 The Ganges river dolphin , found in freshwater rivers, is endangered due to water 
pollution and dam construction.  
 These species are critical to maintaining the ba lance of their respective ecosystems. 
 Conservation efforts like protected areas and species -specific programs are in place to 
help protect them. 
Global Examples of Endangered Species  
 Many species worldwide face the threat of extinction due to habitat loss and human 
activities. 
 The Giant panda from China is endangered because of shrinking bamboo forests and low 
reproduction rates. 
 The Black rhino , native to Africa, is critically endangered mainly due to poaching for its 
horn. 


=== page 10 ===
 The Orangutan, found in Southeas t Asia, is threatened by deforestation and illegal pet 
trade. 
 These species are important for maintaining the ecological balance of their habitats.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Global conservation efforts involve habitat protection, anti -poaching laws, and captive 
breeding programs. 
Conservation Strategies for Endangered Species 
 Conservation strategies aim to protect endangered species and restore their populations. 
 Wildlife sanctuaries and national parks  provide safe habitats free from hunting and 
habitat destruction. 
 Captive breeding  programs help increase population numbers and can reintroduce 
animals into the wild. 
 Legal protection, such as the Wildlife Protection Act in India, bans hunting and trade of 
endangered species. 
 Community involvement and education are also vita l for successful conservation. 
 Combined efforts improve survival chances and help maintain ecosystem balance.  
: Endemic Species – Definition 
 Endemic species are organisms that are found in only one specific geographic region and 
nowhere else in the world. 
 This region can be as small as an island or as large as a continent.  
 These species have evolved in isolation and are often highly adapted to their environment. 
 Because of their limited distribution, they are highly vulnerable to extinction.  
 Any changes to their habitat can threaten their entire population.  


=== page 11 ===
 Endemic species are key indicators of regional biodiversity and conservation priorities.  
Importance of Endemic Species  
 Endemic species are irreplaceable components of the ecosystems they inhabit.  
 They often have high conservation value due to their restricted range and uniqueness.  
 These species can be sensitive to environmental changes, making them valuable for 
monitoring ecosystem health. 
 Their presence reflects the evolutionary richness of a region.  
 As indicators of regional biodiversity, endemic species help identify biodiversity hotspots. 
 Protecting them is essential for preserving the genetic and ecological uniqueness of an 
area. 
Examples of Indian Endemic Species  
 India is home to many unique endemic s pecies due to its diverse geography and 
ecosystems. 
 The Nilgiri tahr is a mountain goat found only in the Western Ghats of South India.  
 The Lion-tailed macaque  is a rare primate endemic to the rainforests of the Western 
Ghats. 
 The Andaman teal  is a duck sp ecies found only in the freshwater wetlands of the 
Andaman Islands. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 These species are highly adapted to their native habitats and cannot survive elsewhere.  


=== page 12 ===
 Protecting endemic species helps preserve India ’s ecological heritage and biodiversity. 
: Threats to Endemic Species  
 Endemic species face unique threats due to their limited distribution and specialized 
habitats. 
 Local habitat destruction  from agriculture, deforestation, and urban development can 
wipe out entire populations. 
 Invasive species  compete with or prey on endemic species, often leading to rapid 
declines. 
 Their restricted distribution  means they cannot migrate or adapt easily to changing 
environments. 
 Natural disasters or climate shifts can be devastating for these species.  
 Conservation of endemic species requires focused protection of their specific habitats.  
Rare Species – Definition 
 Rare species are those with small population sizes or limited geographic distribution.  
 Unlike endangered species, they are not currently at high risk of e xtinction. 
 However, their low numbers make them vulnerable to threats and future decline. 
 Rare species may naturally exist in low numbers due to specialized habitat or slow 
reproduction. 
 They often require specific environmental conditions to survive.  
 Monitoring rare species is important to prevent them from becoming endangered.  
Factors Leading to Rarity 
 Several natural and human-made factors contribute to a species becoming rare. 
 Habitat specificity  means a species can only survive in very particular envir onmental 
conditions. 
 Low reproductive rates  make it difficult for populations to grow or recover after 
disturbances. 
 Human interference, such as land use change, hunting, or pollution, reduces population 
sizes. 

=== page 13 ===
 These factors limit distribution, genetic diversity, and survival potential. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Understanding these causes helps design better conservation strategies for rare species.  
 Examples of Rare Species 
 Rare species are found in small numbers and often have a very limited range.  
 The Himalayan quail is a ground -dwelling bird last reliably seen in the 19th century and 
may still exist in remote Himalayan areas. 
 The Malabar civet, once thought extinct, is critically endangered and possibly one of the 
rarest mammals in India. 
 The Gooty tarantula is a vividly colored spider found only in a small forest patch in Andhra 
Pradesh. 
 These species are highly vulnerable to habitat change and human disturbance.  
 Protecting their habitats is essential to prevent them from sliding into extinction.  
Role of Engineers in Species Conservation  
 Engineers play a key role in integrating biodiversity conservation into development 
projects. 
 Sustainable infrastructure planning  minimizes habitat destruction and supports 
coexistence with wildlife. 
 Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs) help identify and mitigate potential threats to 
species before construction begins. 
 Eco-friendly design incorporates green technologies, renewable resources, and minimal 
ecological disruption. 


=== page 14 ===
 Engineers can innovate solutions like wildlife corridors,  green buildings, and water -
efficient systems. 
 Their decisions directly influence habitat preservation, pollution levels, and long -term 
ecosystem health. 
 Interconnection Between Interactions and Species Types  
 Species interactions like predation, mutualism , and competition shape the structure of 
ecosystems. 
 When these interactions are disrupted, species may lose essential support systems like 
food, pollination, or shelter. 
 This disruption can lead to population decline, increasing the risk of endangerment o r 
extinction. 
 For example, the loss of pollinators can threaten plant species, leading to a cascading 
effect on herbivores and predators. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Ecosystems operate through feedback loops, where the decline of one species can trigger 
imbalance in others. 
 Understanding these links helps in designing effective conservation strategies.  
Conclusion 
 Species interactions are essential for maintaining ecological balance and biodiversity.  
 Disruptions in these interactions can lead to species becoming extinct, endan gered, 
endemic, or rare. 
 Each species category reflects the fragility and uniqueness of ecosystems.

 Engineers, scientists, policymakers, and communities  must work together for sustainable

 Protecting biodiversity ensures the health of ecosystems and the well -being of future
